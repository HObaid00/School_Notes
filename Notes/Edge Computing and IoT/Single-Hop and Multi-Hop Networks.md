# Single-Hop and Multi-Hop Networks

In a **single-hop** network, a source can directly reach the destination.

In a **multi-hop** network, packets are forwarded through intermediate nodes.

$$\Large
\text{source} \rightarrow \text{relay node} \rightarrow \text{destination}
$$

Multi-hop communication is useful when direct radio range is limited by distance, power constraints, or obstacles.

The forwarding model is often called **store-and-forward**: an intermediate node receives a packet, stores it briefly, and forwards it to the next hop.

Multi-hop networks extend range but add latency, routing complexity, and energy cost for relay nodes.
