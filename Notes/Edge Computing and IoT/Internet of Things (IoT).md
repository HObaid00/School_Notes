# Internet of Things (IoT)

The **Internet of Things (IoT)** means extending the Internet beyond normal computers, phones, and servers so that everyday physical objects can sense, compute, communicate, and sometimes act on the environment.

An IoT device usually has:

- **Sensors**, which measure something from the physical world.
- **Actuators**, which change something in the physical world.
- **A processor**, often much weaker than a PC processor.
- **A network interface**, often wireless and low-power.
- **A power source**, often a battery.

Examples include smart light bulbs, washing machines, connected cars, environmental sensors, smart agriculture systems, and industrial monitoring devices.

The key idea is:

$$\Large
\text{physical world} \rightarrow \text{sensing} \rightarrow \text{data} \rightarrow
$$

$$\Large
\rightarrow \text{networked computation} \rightarrow \text{action or decision}
$$

For example, a smart smoke detector senses smoke, processes the signal locally, sends an alarm message, and may trigger other devices.
