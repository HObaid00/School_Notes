# Why the Dayton Audio Sound Exciter (Transducer) is Suitable for the Sound Masking Project

The Dayton Audio sound exciter is suitable for the sound masking system because it converts audio signals into structural vibrations that propagate through surfaces such as wooden doors.

The transducer acts as the acoustic output stage:

$$\Large
\text{Audio Signal} \rightarrow \text{Amplifier} \rightarrow \text{Sound Exciter} \rightarrow \text{Door Panel Vibrations}
$$

---

# 1. Surface Vibration-Based Audio Output

Unlike conventional speakers, the sound exciter vibrates the mounting surface directly.

This enables:
- hidden audio transmission,
- distributed sound radiation,
- and structural sound propagation.

The wooden door becomes the sound-radiating surface.

---

# 2. Effective Speech Masking Through Door Panels

The exciter can distribute masking noise across the door surface, improving:
- speech masking coverage,
- structural vibration distribution,
- and acoustic diffusion.

This is beneficial for:
- privacy enhancement,
- and voice intelligibility reduction.

---

# 3. Compact and Concealed Design

The exciter mounts directly onto the rear surface of the door.

Benefits include:
- minimal visual impact,
- hidden installation,
- and improved physical integration.

This supports:
- discreet deployment,
- and real-world IoT installation.

---

# 4. Strong Mid-Frequency Response

The exciter performs well within the critical speech masking region:

$$\Large
250\,Hz \le f \le 4\,kHz
$$

This range contains:
- speech intelligibility information,
- consonant clarity,
- and vocal articulation frequencies.

---

# 5. Improved Acoustic Coupling with Wooden Doors

Wooden doors naturally resonate and transmit vibration energy efficiently.

The exciter leverages:
- panel resonance,
- structural conduction,
- and distributed vibration modes.

This improves:
- masking coverage,
- and vibration efficiency.

---

# 6. Low Physical Footprint

The exciter requires:
- minimal enclosure space,
- no speaker grille,
- and no acoustic cavity.

This simplifies:
- embedded integration,
- and installation flexibility.

---

# 7. Reduced Directional Audio Leakage

Because sound radiates through the door surface:
- audio becomes more diffuse,
- less directional,
- and harder to localize.

This improves:
- perceptual masking,
- and acoustic privacy.

---

# 8. Compatibility with Class-D Amplifiers

The exciter can be driven efficiently using:
- low-power Class-D amplifiers,
- embedded audio systems,
- and battery-powered devices.

This improves:
- energy efficiency,
- and portable deployment capability.

---

# 9. Structural Transfer Function Analysis

The door modifies transmitted frequencies according to:

$$\Large
H(f)=\frac{Y(f)}{X(f)}
$$

Where:
- $X(f)$ = input signal spectrum
- $Y(f)$ = measured door output spectrum
- $H(f)$ = acoustic transfer function

This allows analysis of:
- resonance,
- damping,
- and masking performance.

---

# Technical Justification

The Dayton Audio sound exciter was selected because it enables efficient surface-based acoustic masking through structural vibration of wooden door panels, providing concealed audio output, effective speech-band transmission, and compact integration for adaptive privacy-focused sound masking systems.
