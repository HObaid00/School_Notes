# ⭐ What is a Robot?

Robots are **physical agents** that perform tasks by manipulating the physical world.

- Environment sensed by **sensors**
- Environment affected by **effectors / actuators**
- Controlled by an **intelligent agent**

---

# ⭐ Primary Robot Categories

- **Manipulators (robot arms)**  
  Physically anchored (e.g., factory assembly line)

- **Mobile robots**  
  Move using wheels, legs, or similar mechanisms

- **Mobile manipulators**  
  Combination of both (e.g., humanoids)

---

# ⭐ Why Robotics is Challenging

- Partially observable, stochastic, dynamic, continuous environments  
- Real-time constraints  
- Time-consuming real-world testing  
- Interdisciplinary field

---

# ⭐ Sensor Categories

## Passive Sensors
Detect effects generated by environment  
Examples: camera, infrared, GPS, light sensor

## Active Sensors
Emit energy and measure reflection  
Examples: SONAR, RADAR, LIDAR

## Proprioceptive Sensors
Sense robot’s internal state  
Examples: angle sensor, force sensor, acceleration sensor

---

# ⭐ Degrees of Freedom (DOF)

Number of independent parameters defining configuration.

Single rigid body in 3D:

- 3 translational: $(x,y,z)$  
- 3 rotational: (roll, pitch, yaw)

Total:

$$
DOF = 6
$$

---

# ⭐ Types of Joints

- Revolute → 1 rotational DOF  
- Prismatic → 1 translational DOF  
- Helical → $d = h\theta$  
- Cylindrical → 1 rotation + 1 translation  
- Spherical → 3 rotational DOF  
- Planar → 3 translational DOF  

---

# ⭐ Manipulators

- Configuration specified by joint values
- Minimum DOF to position end effector arbitrarily:

$$
DOF \ge 6
$$

Human arms have **more than 6 DOF**.

---

# ⭐ Holonomic vs Nonholonomic Robots

Holonomic:

$$
\text{effective DOF} = \text{controllable DOF}
$$

Nonholonomic:

$$
\text{effective DOF} > \text{controllable DOF}
$$

Example: Car  
- DOF: $(x,y,\theta)$  
- Controls: steering, throttle  
⇒ nonholonomic

# ⭐ State Estimation (Filtering)

State:

$$
X_t = \text{robot state + environment state}
$$

Observation:

$$
Z_t
$$

Action:

$$
A_t
$$

Recursive belief update:

$$
P(X_{t+1}|z_{1:t+1},a_{1:t}) =
\alpha P(z_{t+1}|X_{t+1})
\int P(X_{t+1}|x_t,a_t) P(x_t|z_{1:t},a_{1:t-1}) dx_t
$$

---

# ⭐ Localization

Finding the robot’s position.

Assume:

- 2D surface  
- Known map  

Robot state:

$$
(x,y,\theta)
$$

---

# ⭐ Deterministic Motion Model

Given translational velocity $v_t$ and rotational velocity $\omega_t$:

$$
\hat{X}_{t+1} =
X_t +
\begin{bmatrix}
v_t \Delta t \cos\theta_t\\
v_t \Delta t \sin\theta_t\\
\omega_t \Delta t
\end{bmatrix}
$$

---

# ⭐ Stochastic Motion Model

$$
P(X_{t+1}|X_t,v_t,\omega_t)
=
\mathcal{N}(\hat{X}_{t+1},\Sigma_x)
$$

---

# ⭐ Landmark Sensor Model

Robot state:

$$
x_t = (s_t^x,s_t^y,\theta_t)
$$

Landmark:

$$
(s_i^x,s_i^y)
$$

Distance:

$$
d = \sqrt{(s_t^x-s_i^x)^2+(s_t^y-s_i^y)^2}
$$

Bearing:

$$
\phi = \arctan\left(\frac{s_i^y-s_t^y}{s_i^x-s_t^x}\right)-\theta_t
$$

Observation:

$$
\hat{z}_t = 
\begin{bmatrix}
d\\
\phi
\end{bmatrix}
$$

# ⭐ Types of Motion

- Point-to-point motion  
- Compliant motion (contact with environment)

---

# ⭐ Configuration Space (C-Space)

Represent robot by **joint values**.

- Free space → feasible  
- Occupied space → collision

Planning done in C-space.

---

# ⭐ Kinematics

**Forward kinematics:** joint values → end-effector pose  
**Inverse kinematics:** desired pose → joint values  

Inverse kinematics often **non-unique**.

---

# ⭐ Path Planning Methods

## Cell Decomposition
- Discretize space
- Graph search (e.g., A*)

Pros: simple  
Cons: exponential in dimension

## Skeletonization

- Voronoi graphs  
- Probabilistic Roadmaps (PRM)

---

# ⭐ Planning Under Uncertainty

- Online replanning  
- MDP: fully observable  
- POMDP: partially observable  

---

# ⭐ Robust Motion Planning

Works for all values within bounds (no probabilities).

Used in fine-motion tasks (peg-in-hole).

---

# ⭐ Control of Movements

## Open Loop
No feedback

## Closed Loop
Uses feedback

---

# ⭐ PID Control

Error:

$$
e(t) = y(t) - r(t)
$$

P:

$$
u(t)=K_P e(t)
$$

PD:

$$
u(t)=K_P e(t)+K_D \frac{de(t)}{dt}
$$

PID:

$$
u(t)=K_P e(t)+K_I \int e(t)dt + K_D \frac{de(t)}{dt}
$$

---

# ⭐ Potential-Field Control

- Attractive force to goal  
- Repulsive force from obstacles  

Problem: local minima

---

# ⭐ Reactive Control

- No model
- Reflex-like behavior
- Often combined with PID

---

# ⭐ Learning Control Strategies

Learn dynamics from demonstrations instead of modeling.
